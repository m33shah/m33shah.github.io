What Dua Lipa Can Teach Us About Brand Building

By Michael Shah

We often hear that authenticity sells.

I believe it does.

However, authenticity alone is not enough. The brands that leave a lasting impression combine authenticity with consistency. People rarely build trust after just one interview, campaign, or social media post. Trust develops when every interaction reinforces the same identity over time.

Few public figures demonstrate this better than Dua Lipa.

While she is one of the world's biggest music artists, her success as a brand extends far beyond her music. Whether she is attending a football match, travelling with friends, reading a book, dancing at a festival, or representing luxury fashion, the experience feels genuine because it feels consistent. Her image is stylish without being unapproachable, confident without appearing manufactured, and aspirational without feeling unattainable. As a result, partnerships with brands such as Porsche, Bulgari, Chanel, YSL Beauty, Puma, and Nespresso feel credible because they complement the identity she has built rather than attempt to redefine it.

The same principle extends well beyond the music industry.

Ryan Reynolds has built a personal brand around humour, self-awareness, and creativity. Those qualities remain consistent whether he is promoting a film, speaking about Wrexham AFC, launching a business, or appearing in an advertisement. Audiences know what to expect, which makes every new partnership feel authentic rather than transactional.

Gordon Ramsay offers another compelling example. His reputation was built on demanding excellence in the kitchen, yet audiences also know him for his passion for food, his willingness to teach, and his sense of humour. Whether he is mentoring aspiring chefs, travelling to explore local cuisine, or sharing simple recipes online, the core of his personality never changes. His consistency has transformed him from a celebrity chef into a global brand.

Although these examples come from entertainment, the lesson applies to every professional.

Many people believe personal branding is about creating the perfect LinkedIn profile, writing polished posts, or using the latest buzzwords. In reality, strong personal brands are built through repetition. People remember what they consistently experience, not what they occasionally see.

Marketing has always been about creating clear associations in the minds of consumers. The same principle applies to individuals. When people consistently associate someone with expertise, creativity, integrity, or leadership, that reputation becomes an asset. It creates trust, opens doors, and attracts opportunities long before a conversation ever begins.

Authenticity may capture attention.

Consistency builds brands.

The most successful brands, whether they belong to a global artist, an entrepreneur, or a young professional, are not built by trying to be everything to everyone.
