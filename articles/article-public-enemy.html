What Public Enemy Taught Me About Creativity

By Michael Shah

Listening to Public Enemy can be exhausting. Their music is loud, relentless, chaotic, and at times almost overwhelming. Yet the more I listen, the more I appreciate just how brilliant and ahead of their time they were.

What fascinates me most is how they brought together so many different influences. Working with the Bomb Squad, Public Enemy borrowed from funk, soul, jazz, rock, and seemingly every corner of popular culture. James Brown samples, distorted guitars, sirens, scratching, and competing rhythms were thrown together in ways that should not have worked. Somehow, they did.

Take Welcome to the Terrordome, from their 1990 album Fear of a Black Planet. The song barely gives you a moment to breathe. So much is happening that it is hard to know where to focus your attention. Even a laugh sounds like Homer Simpson buried in the mix. Who would think to put something like that on a hip-hop record?

And that is what I find so interesting about their approach. They could hear possibilities in sounds that most people would overlook. Rather than being confined to one genre or influence, they took inspiration from practically everywhere and made it their own.

We sometimes talk about creativity as though every original idea comes from nowhere. But Public Enemy reminds me that originality can also come from curiosity, experimentation, and an ability to connect things that appear completely unrelated.

Their music was not always easy to listen to, and it was not trying to be. The Bomb Squad embraced the noise, the contradictions, and the sheer density. In doing so, they helped create a sound that influenced generations of artists.

Public Enemy did not invent every sound they used. Their genius was in hearing what nobody else could hear when those sounds came together.
