Crossing the Firing Line: What We Can Learn From Our Rivals

By Michael Shah

Sometimes the best lessons come from the places we least want to look. As a Tottenham supporter, praising Arsenal does not come naturally. However, there is something to be learned from how they have built their team, strengthened their brand, and trusted potential.

When Arsenal appointed Mikel Arteta, they took a gamble. He had worked alongside Pep Guardiola at Manchester City, but he had never managed a senior team himself. The journey was far from perfect. Arsenal finished eighth in back-to-back Premier League seasons, and there were plenty of questions about whether Arteta was the right person for the job. Instead of abandoning the project, the club gave him the time and resources to build around a clear vision. They trusted the process and believed in what he could become rather than expecting the finished product immediately.

That lesson applies to any organization. Building a great team sometimes requires looking beyond the obvious choice. Experience matters, but so do ambition, potential, curiosity, and the desire to improve. Someone who does not check every box today could become exceptional when given the opportunity and trust to grow.

Arsenal has taken a similar approach to its brand. Its work with Adidas has produced advertising that embraces North London, the club’s history, its supporters, and its culture while presenting them in creative and modern ways. The partnership demonstrates how two established brands can create something new without compromising what makes either of them recognizable.
The marketing feels authentic because there is a clear identity behind it.

Whether you are building a football team, a company, a department, or a brand, success rarely happens overnight. It requires a clear vision, the right people, patience, and the confidence to trust the process when results are not immediate. Every successful leader was once someone’s gamble. Sometimes the best decision is not finding the perfect person. It is believing that someone can become one.

Even if learning that lesson means crossing the firing line.
